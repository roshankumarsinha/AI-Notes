# Module 1: Meet Claude

## What is Claude?

### Overview

Claude is more than a chatbot. Think of it as a "thinking partner" that works through problems with you, instead of just answering quick questions.

### Guiding principles

Claude is trained to be helpful, honest, and harmless. This approach is called "Constitutional AI" — it means Claude follows a set of values so it avoids harmful, unfair, or dishonest responses, while still trying to be as useful as possible.

### Steerable and collaborative

You can guide Claude's tone and personality to fit what you need. This makes conversations feel natural, and you get better results with less effort.

### Access anywhere

Claude is available on the web, on desktop apps, and on mobile apps. Your chats, projects, and preferences stay in sync no matter which device you use.

### Key skills

- **Writing**: Helps draft and improve emails, social posts, and reports.
- **Research**: Can read and understand very long documents (500+ pages), making it useful for digging into complex material.
- **Coding**: Writes, debugs, and explains code in many programming languages.
- **Problem-solving**: Can reason step-by-step through hard questions using a slower, more careful "Thinking" mode.
- **Learning support**: A "Learning" mode guides you to think through answers yourself, rather than just giving you the solution.

### Ways to access Claude

- **Claude.ai** (web/desktop/mobile app) – main way most people chat with Claude.
- **Claude Code** – built for developers; can edit files and run commands.
- **@Claude in Slack** – chat with Claude inside Slack channels and threads.
- **Claude Design** – turns your ideas or sketches into working prototypes.
- **Claude for Microsoft 365** – adds Claude inside Word, Excel, PowerPoint, and Outlook.

## Your First Conversation with Claude

### Overview

Claude is a powerful collaborator that amplifies what you can do. Claude brings AI intelligence, but you bring the context and expertise that makes the work meaningful. Talk to Claude like you would a coworker — naturally, concisely, and conversationally.

### Writing effective prompts

A good prompt usually has three parts:

- **Setting the stage**: Explain your role, your goal, and any background Claude should know.
- **Defining the task**: Say exactly what you want Claude to do (write, analyze, build, etc.).
- **Specifying rules**: Describe the tone, style, or format you want, and give examples if you have them.

Example: telling Claude you're a marketing lead who needs a market research report, with citations, structured into specific sections — that prompt covers all three parts above.

### Adding context with files

You can upload files like PDFs, Word docs, spreadsheets, text files, and images. Claude will read and understand them. Useful examples:

- Upload a document and ask for a summary.
- Share an image and ask Claude to describe it.
- Attach a spreadsheet and ask Claude to find trends.
- Upload code and ask Claude to explain or debug it.

### Iterating on responses

Conversations work best as a back-and-forth, not a single one-off question. If a response isn't quite right, you can:

- Ask a follow-up question for more detail.
- Give feedback ("make this more casual").
- Redirect Claude if it misunderstood you.
- Edit and resend a previous message using the pencil icon.

### Personalizing Claude

- **Memory**: Claude automatically remembers useful context (your role, preferences, past decisions) so you don't repeat yourself. You can review or delete this anytime in Settings.
- **Styles**: Let you set how Claude communicates (concise, formal, explanatory, or a custom style), applied across all your chats.

## Getting Better Results

### Common challenges and fixes

- **Response is too generic**: Add specific details (audience, role, situation) instead of a vague ask.
- **Response is too long/short**: Tell Claude the exact length you want (e.g., "under 100 words" or "two paragraphs").
- **Claude didn't follow my format**: Describe or show the structure you want (e.g., "use bullet points with bold headers").
- **Claude gives confident but wrong info**: For important facts, double-check independently, ask Claude to cite sources, or turn on web search.
- **Wrong tone**: Describe the tone you want in plain words, or give an example of the style.

### The iteration mindset

Your first prompt is rarely perfect — treat it as the start of a conversation, not a final answer.

- Review the first draft, then refine it.
- Give specific feedback (not just "make it shorter," but exactly what to cut or change).
- If a chat goes off track, sometimes it's easier to start a new one.

### What is AI Fluency?

AI Fluency means knowing how to collaborate well with AI, not just how to use the tools. It has four parts (the "4D Framework"):

- **Delegation**: Deciding what tasks go to you vs. to AI.
- **Description**: Clearly explaining what you want from Claude.
- **Discernment**: Checking Claude's answers carefully for quality and accuracy.
- **Diligence**: Using AI responsibly and ethically, staying accountable for the results.

### Evaluating Claude for your work ("evals")

Evals help you test how well Claude performs on tasks you care about. A simple approach:

1. Gather 5–10 real examples of work you've done before.
2. Write prompts that would create similar results.
3. Compare Claude's output to your own examples — check accuracy, tone, and what's missing.
4. Adjust your prompts or add examples based on what you learn.

## How You'll Work with Claude on Your Desktop

### Three shapes of work

The Claude desktop app supports three different ways of working:

1. **Turn by turn** – You and Claude go back and forth in conversation.
2. **Handing work off** – You describe the end result, and Claude plans and does the whole task, then returns with a finished output.
3. **Building software** – Claude works directly inside a codebase (mainly for developers).

### 1. Turn by turn (lives in "Chat")

Best for: brainstorming, drafting, or quick questions where your next question depends on Claude's last answer.

- Quick entry: double-tap Option key (Mac) to pull up Claude instantly.
- Can use screenshots, window sharing, and dictation (Mac).
- Can connect to local tools via desktop connectors.

### 2. Handing work off (lives in "Cowork")

Best for: multi-step tasks, tasks that produce a real file (doc, sheet, deck), tasks that pull from multiple tools, or tasks that run on a schedule.

- Claude may ask clarifying questions first and shows you its plan.
- You can watch progress and steer at any point.
- Claude pauses for approval before sensitive actions (like sending an email).
- Features include: local folder access (saves files back to your folder), scheduled tasks, subagents (splitting big jobs into parallel workers), Projects (grouped tasks with shared files/memory), browser use (Claude navigates websites for you), computer use (Claude can operate your computer directly, asking permission first), and plugins (ready-made skill bundles for specific roles).
- Available on Pro, Max, Team, and Enterprise plans.

### 3. Building software (lives in the "Code" tab)

For developers: Claude can read, write, and test code directly in a codebase, and run terminal commands. You choose:

- **Local**: work directly with files on your computer.
- **Cloud**: connect a GitHub repo; sessions keep running even if you close the app.
  You can also choose how independently Claude works: manually approve each change, auto-accept edits, or have Claude plan first.

### Choosing the right shape

Ask yourself: is this a quick back-and-forth (Chat), a full task that ends
in a real deliverable or spans tools/schedules (Cowork), or coding work in a codebase (Code tab)?

# Module 2: Organizing your work and knowledge

## Introduction to Projects

### What are Projects?

Projects are self-contained workspaces in Claude. Each one has its own memory, chat history, uploaded documents ("knowledge base"), and custom instructions — like a dedicated folder for a specific work area.

### When to use a Project

Good for ongoing work, not one-off questions. Use a project when you have:

- Reference materials you'll reuse often (reports, meeting notes, data).
- Consistent rules for how Claude should respond (tone, format, sources).
- A team that needs to share the same context.

### Setting up a project

1. **Create it**: Go to Projects in the sidebar, click "+ New Project," give it a name and short description, and set it to private or shared.
2. **Add instructions**: Tell Claude how to behave in this project — context, process, tone, and specific rules. These apply to every chat inside the project.
3. **Build the knowledge base**: Upload documents (PDF, DOCX, CSV, TXT, etc.) or connect Google Drive. Claude will reference these files in every chat inside the project. Use clear, descriptive file names so Claude can find the right information.

### Handling large knowledge bases

If you upload a lot of documents, Claude uses a method called Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG) — instead of loading everything at once, it searches your files and pulls out only what's relevant. This lets aproject hold up to 10x more content while keeping good response quality.
Imagine Claude's "memory" for a conversation is like a desk. Normally, when you upload a few documents to a project, Claude puts all of them on the desk and can look at everything at once while answering your question. This works fine when you don't have too many files.
But what if you keep adding more and more documents, until they can no longer all fit on that desk? Instead of failing or giving up, Claude switches strategy. Rather than trying to keep every single document open in front of it at once, it acts more like a librarian: when you ask a question, it quickly searches through all your uploaded files, finds only the pages or sections that are actually relevant to your question, and pulls just those onto the desk to use for its answer.
This searching method is called "Retrieval Augmented Generation," or RAG for short. So, instead of reading everything every time, Claude looks up only the relevant parts, kind of like using an index in a book instead of reading the whole book cover to cover.

### Collaboration (Team/Enterprise plans)

You can share a project with teammates using three permission levels:

- **Can view**: See and chat, but can't make changes.
- **Can edit**: Can update instructions, knowledge, and members.
- **Owner**: Full control, including who can access the project.

### Best practices

- Start with one focused project rather than trying to cover everything.
- Keep the knowledge base up to date.
- Write clear, specific instructions.
- Name files descriptively and group related files together.
- Reference specific documents by name when asking questions.

## Creating with Artifacts

### What are Artifacts?

Artifacts are standalone outputs Claude creates in a separate window next to your chat — like a working website, chart, or document — instead of burying content inside the conversation text.

Claude usually creates one when the content is:

- Fairly long or self-contained (often 15+ lines).
- Something you'll want to edit, reuse, or reference later.
- Complex enough to stand on its own outside the chat.

### Common types of artifacts

- **Documents**: text-heavy content like reports, notes, or blog posts.
- **Code snippets**: working code in any programming language.
- **HTML pages**: full mini web pages (landing pages, demos, prototypes).
- **SVG images**: logos, icons, and illustrations.
- **Mermaid diagrams**: flowcharts, org charts, timelines, etc.
- **React components**: interactive tools like calculators or dashboards.

Note: Word docs, Excel sheets, PowerPoint files, and PDFs are handled separately (as downloadable files), not as artifacts.

### Creating an artifact

Just describe what you want in conversation — Claude decides whether to show it as an artifact. If it doesn't happen automatically, you can ask directly: "Create this as an artifact."

Inside the artifact window, you can:

- Switch between a preview and the underlying code.
- Copy the content.
- Download it as a file.

### Sharing and publishing

- **Copy/download**: for personal use.
- **Share within your org** (Team/Enterprise): keeps it internal, requires team login.
- **Publish publicly** (Free/Pro/Max): anyone with the link can view and interact with the artifact without a Claude account and even "remix" it(remix requires claude account) — but it won't show up in search engines, and your chat itself stays private.

### Tips for better artifacts

- Be specific about what you want (mention features, not just a general idea).
- Describe who the artifact is for.
- Build it up one change at a time, rather than everything at once.
- If Claude replies in plain chat instead, just ask for it as an artifact.

## Working with Skills

### What are Skills?

Skills are folders of instructions, scripts, and resources that Claude loads to get better at specific tasks. Think of them as "expertise packages" that teach Claude how to repeat a task the same way every time.

### Two types of Skills

- **Anthropic Skills**: Built and maintained by Anthropic (e.g., creating Excel, Word, PowerPoint, and PDF files). These run automatically — you don't need to set anything up.
- **Custom Skills**: Created by you or your organization for specific workflows, like applying brand guidelines or following a compliance checklist.

### Enabling Skills

Currently available (as a preview) on Pro, Max, Team, and Enterprise plans. To use them:

1. Go to Settings > Capabilities.
2. Turn on "Code execution and file creation" (Skills need this to run).
3. Scroll to the Skills section and toggle individual skills on/off.

### Using Skills in practice

You usually don't need to think about Skills — Claude picks the right one automatically based on your request (e.g., "Create an Excel spreadsheet with formulas" or "Turn these notes into a PowerPoint"). Claude also can work directly with uploaded files like slides or contracts to create updated versions for you to download.

### Security note

Because Skills can run code, only use custom Skills from trusted sources, and review any external skill before use.

### Creating your own custom Skill

You can create a Skill just by chatting with Claude:

1. Tell Claude what workflow you want turned into a skill.
2. Answer Claude's questions about what "good output" looks like.
3. Upload any templates or examples you have.
4. Save the skill — Claude builds the file structure for you.
   You can find and edit your Skills under the "Customize" tab.

### Skills vs. Projects

- **Projects** store knowledge (reference materials Claude can look up).
- **Skills** store process (the steps Claude should follow to do a task).
  They work well together: a project can hold the information, while a skill defines how to use that information consistently.

# Module 3: Expanding Claude's reach

## Connecting Your Tools

### What are Connectors?

Connectors let Claude access the same tools and data you use every day (like Google Drive, Slack, or Notion), instead of starting from scratch every conversation. Depending on the permissions you grant, Claude can search, read, and sometimes take actions in those apps directly from your chat.

### How connectors work (MCP)

Connectors are powered by something called the Model Context Protocol (MCP) — think of it like a universal plug (like USB-C) that lets Claude connect to many different apps using one consistent system.

### Two types of connectors

- **Web connectors**: link Claude to cloud services (Gmail, Notion, Slack, Asana, Stripe, etc.).
- **Desktop extensions**: run locally through the Claude Desktop app, giving access to local files and native apps on your computer.

### Setting up a connector

**Web connector**:

1. Go to claude.ai/directory, or click "+" > Connectors in a chat.
2. Click "Connect" on the tool you want.
3. Log in to that service.
4. Review and approve the permissions requested.
5. Test it by asking Claude something simple about that tool.

**Desktop extension**:

1. Install the Claude Desktop app.
2. Go to Settings > Extensions.
3. Browse and install the extension you want.
4. Follow any extra setup steps.

### Example uses

- **Project management** (Asana, Linear): "What tasks are due this week?"
- **Communication** (Slack, Gmail): "Find the email about the vendor contract.", "Draft a reply to the latest message in the #marketing channel"
- **Documentation** (Notion, Drive): "Summarize last week's meeting notes."
- **Business tools** (Stripe, Salesforce): "Show revenue trends this quarter."

### Security and permissions

- Claude only gets scoped (limited) access — just what the connector needs. Permissions are specific to what the connector needs and you can toggle individual permissions on and off within each application's menu.
- Claude can only see what you can see; it doesn't get extra access beyond your own account.
- You can disconnect any connector at any time.
- Only use connectors from trusted sources.

## Enterprise Search (Not applicable to Free, Pro, or Max plans)

_(Available on Team and Enterprise plans only, must be turned on by an admin)_

### What is Enterprise Search?

It adds an "Ask {Your Org Name}" option in the sidebar — like a pre-built project for your whole company, already loaded with your organization's knowledge (documents, chats, emails). It's built specifically to help you find and combine information spread across your company's tools.

### What can you ask?

- **Catching up**: "What happened while I was out?"
- **Policy questions**: "What's our remote work policy?"
- **Research**: "Why do customers choose competitors over us?"
- **Onboarding**: "How does our authentication system work?"
- **Project tracking**: "What were the key decisions from last week's meeting?"

Claude searches across all your connected tools (like SharePoint, Slack, Gmail, Google Drive) and gives one combined answer, always citing where the information came from.

### Setting it up

**Admins (Owners)** must set it up first:

1. Click "Ask Your Org" in the sidebar, then "Set up for your org."
2. Connect tools for Documents (e.g., Drive/SharePoint) and Chat (e.g., Slack/Teams). Email is optional but recommended.
3. Name and describe the project, then finish setup.

**Regular users**, after admin setup:

1. Click the "Ask {Org Name}" project in the sidebar.
2. Follow the onboarding steps and log in to each connected service.
3. Start asking questions.

### Is it safe?

Yes — Enterprise Search only shows you information you already have permission to see in the original tool. Your conversations stay private and aren't stored separately.

## Research for Deep Dives

### What is Research?

Research turns Claude into a systematic investigator instead of a simple Q&A assistant. It explores your question from multiple angles, running many searches across the web (and your connected tools), and compiles everything into one thorough report. It takes minutes rather than seconds, because it's doing many searches, not just one.

### When to use Research

Use it for: comprehensive reports, comparing options (like vendors or competitors), deep investigations that would normally take hours, and reports where you want citations to verify the facts.

Use something else instead when:

- **Quick web search**: for a simple, single fact (like today's exchange rate). The answer requires only one or two sources.
- **Thinking mode**: for reasoning through math, code, or logic problems that don't need outside information.
- **Enterprise Search**: for questions about your own company's internal documents and chats, not the public web.

### How Research works

1. **Plans its approach**: Breaks your request into parts before searching and plans how to investigate the different angles of your question.
2. **Runs multiple searches**: Rather than running a single search, Claude conducts many searches that build on each other.Each new search builds on what it already found.
3. **Synthesizes findings**: Combines everything into one organized report from different sources.
4. **Cites sources**: Every claim links back to where it came from, so you can check it yourself.

### How to use it

1. Click the "+" button in the chat, then select "Research."
2. Enter your question and submit.
3. Claude works in the background and shows progress as it searches.
   Note: web search must be turned on for Research to work.

### Tips for better Research prompts

- Be specific about your goal, not just a broad topic. Instead of "Tell me about the EV market," try "Analyze the electric vehicle battery market—identify key players, technology trends, and supply chain challenges that might affect investment decisions."
- Ask for a specific structure (e.g., sections you want covered).
- Include constraints like budget, timeline, or location.
- If unsure how to phrase it, ask Claude to help you write the prompt first.

### With connected tools

If you've connected Google Workspace or similar tools, Research can also pull from your emails, calendar, and documents alongside web results — for example, researching people you're meeting with next week based on your calendar.

# Module 4: Putting it all together

## Other Ways to Work with Claude

### Overview

Claude.ai is just one way of working with Claude. Claude is also available in several specialized tools designed to meet you where you already work, each tailored to specific workflows and use cases.

### Claude Code

An agentic coding tool that works in your terminal, IDE, browser, or Slack. It understands your codebase, executes commands, and handles entire development workflows through natural language.

**When to use:**

- Building features by describing requirements in plain English (Claude writes code, runs tests, creates commits)
- Debugging by pasting error messages for Claude to analyze against your codebase
- Navigating an unfamiliar codebase by asking questions
- Automating tedious tasks (lint errors, merge conflicts, release notes)
- Preferring to work in your terminal alongside existing dev tools

### @Claude

Brings Claude directly into Slack — tag @Claude in any channel or thread to get help or pull Slack context into a conversation.

**When to use:**

- Drafting responses, summarizing threads, breaking down discussions
- Preparing for meetings by pulling relevant conversations/documents
- Onboarding to a new team by reviewing channel history
- Handing off coding tasks from a bug report (spins up a Claude Code session)
- Getting quick answers on trends, technical concepts, or company info

### Claude Design

A dedicated space for turning ideas into working interfaces — describe what you want, or start from a sketch/screenshot, and Claude builds an interactive prototype.

**When to use:**

- Going from a brief, sketch, or screenshot to a working UI prototype without code
- Generating and comparing multiple design variations
- Iterating on layout/copy/interactions via conversation
- Producing prototypes aligned with your team's design system

### Claude for Excel

Brings Claude into Excel via a sidebar to analyze, understand, and modify spreadsheets through conversation.

**When to use:**

- Understanding formulas/calculation flows across a complex workbook
- Updating assumptions while preserving formula dependencies
- Debugging errors like #REF!, #VALUE!, or circular references
- Creating new spreadsheets or populating templates
- Building pivot tables or charts quickly

### Claude for PowerPoint

Brings Claude into PowerPoint via a sidebar to draft, edit, and restructure presentations while keeping your template/branding intact.

**When to use:**

- Turning an outline/document into a first-draft deck
- Rewriting or tightening slide copy, adding speaker notes
- Restructuring an existing deck (reordering, splitting, merging slides)
- Applying consistent formatting across the deck
- Getting visual/layout suggestions

### Claude for Word

Brings Claude into Word via a sidebar to draft, revise, and restructure documents, working with tracked changes/comments and connected sources.

**When to use:**

- Turning an outline into a structured first draft
- Revising a section for tone, tightness, or structure in place
- Responding to reviewer comments/tracked changes
- Grounding a draft in connected source material

### Claude for Outlook

Brings Claude into your inbox via a sidebar to triage mail, draft context-aware replies, and summarize long email chains.

**Availability:** Currently in beta; installed separately from other Microsoft 365 add-ins.

### Claude in Chrome

A browser extension adding Claude as a sidebar in Chrome — can observe your work and take actions directly in the browser.

**When to use:**

- Summarizing articles/research/web pages while browsing
- Drafting email responses or managing your inbox
- Automating repetitive form-filling
- Testing website features or navigating multi-step workflows
- Maintaining context across tabs/tasks (e.g., CRMs, dashboards)

**Important note:** Currently in public beta. Recommended for low-risk tasks on trusted sites; asks permission before high-risk actions (e.g., purchasing, sharing personal data); financial services and adult content sites are blocked by default.
